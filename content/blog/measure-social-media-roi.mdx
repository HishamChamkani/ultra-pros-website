"What's the ROI of social?" is the question that makes most marketers reach for a slide full of impressions. But reach and likes aren't returns — and everyone in the room knows it. Here's how to measure social in a way that actually holds up.

## Decide what a result looks like first

You can't measure ROI without agreeing on the "R." For most businesses it's one of three things: leads, sales, or booked calls. Pick the one that maps to revenue and make every channel accountable to it.

## Track the whole path, not just the last click

Social rarely gets the last click — it plants the seed that a search or an email later closes. Judge it only on last-click attribution and you'll defund the thing feeding your whole funnel.

> Social is often the reason someone searched your name in the first place. Measure its influence, not just its final touch.

## The metrics that matter

- **Cost per result** — what you spent to produce a lead, sale, or booking.
- **Assisted conversions** — deals social touched on the way to closing.
- **Audience growth of the right people** — followers who fit your buyer, not vanity numbers.
- **Save and share rate** — the strongest signal that content actually landed.

## Close the loop

Add tracked links, use a consistent UTM structure, and connect your social data to your CRM. Once you can see a follower become a customer, the ROI conversation stops being a debate.

---

Measured properly, social stops being the channel you can't justify and becomes the one you can't cut. The difference is entirely in how you count.
