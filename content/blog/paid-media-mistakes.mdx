Most underperforming ad accounts aren't broken in some dramatic way. They're leaking, slowly, quietly, in places the default dashboard never flags. By the time the monthly report lands, the money's already gone.

Here are the five leaks we find most often when we audit a new account, and exactly how to plug them.

## 1. You're optimizing for the wrong event

If your campaigns optimize for "add to cart" or "lead" but your business runs on qualified sales, the algorithm is doing precisely what you asked, just not what you need. It will happily find you the cheapest cart-adders on the internet.

**Fix:** optimize for the event closest to revenue that still has enough volume to learn from. If purchases are too sparse, use a high-intent proxy and validate it against actual sales monthly.

## 2. Broken or double-counted conversion tracking

Duplicate pixels, misfiring tags, and iOS signal loss mean the numbers in your ad platform rarely match reality. Scaling on bad data just buys you more bad data.

> If you can't trust the number, you can't trust the decision. Fix tracking before you touch budget.

## 3. Creative that never gets tested

Running one ad and calling it a campaign is the fastest way to plateau. Creative is the biggest lever in modern paid media, and it fatigues.

**Fix:** always have three to five concepts in rotation, and retire the losers on a schedule, not a hunch.

## 4. No account structure discipline

Twenty overlapping campaigns bidding against your own audiences will inflate costs and starve your best performers of budget. Consolidate, and let the algorithm learn.

## 5. Set-and-forget budgets

Spend should follow performance, not the calendar. Flat monthly budgets ignore the days, offers, and audiences that are actually working.

**Fix:** review pacing weekly, shift budget toward winners, and protect your cost-per-acquisition target as you scale.

---

Fixing even two of these usually moves the needle within a month. If you're not sure which leaks are costing you most, that's exactly the kind of thing a quick audit uncovers.
